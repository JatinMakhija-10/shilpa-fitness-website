01 — Executive Summary
The current website has a solid foundation: the services are understandable, the target audience is
visible, and there are clear ways to contact the studio. The main weakness is not a lack of information
— it is how that information is packaged and converted into trust.
At present, much of the copy feels interchangeable with other wellness/Pilates websites. The site
describes benefits such as strength, confidence, flexibility and wellness, but gives the visitor relatively
few reasons to believe that Shilpa specifically is the right coach.
The result is a website that informs a visitor, but does not consistently move them through the
sequence: “This is for me → I trust her → I understand the experience → I see proof → I want to book.”
The recommended revamp should therefore focus on positioning, proof, class experience, clearer
pricing, stronger local SEO, better service pages and one consistent booking journey.

02 — Hero Section
Problem: The headline “Transform Your Body & Confidence with Pilates & Face Yoga” is clear but highly
generic. It could belong to almost any Pilates/wellness studio.
Recommended fix: Replace generic wellness language with a sharper value proposition: who the studio
is for, what makes the experience different, where it is, and one clear primary CTA.
03 — Too Little Shilpa
Problem: The business is built around a coach, but the site does not make Shilpa’s story, methodology,
qualifications and personality feel central enough. The “Meet Shilpa” section reads like a generic bio.
Recommended fix: Make the coach the differentiator: certifications, experience, philosophy,
specialisations, teaching approach, real photographs and a stronger personal story.
04 — AI / Template-Like Copy
Problem: Repeated phrases such as “stronger,” “more confident,” “natural glow,” “improve posture,”
and “feel your best” are broad wellness phrases. They do not create a distinctive brand voice.
Recommended fix: Rewrite the copy around real customer situations, real class experiences and
Shilpa’s actual approach. Human-first writing, SEO second.
05 — Audience Section
Problem: The site tries to address beginners, working professionals, moms, pregnant women, people
with pain, people who dislike gyms and people seeking face yoga. The targeting becomes broad rather
than specific.
Recommended fix: Prioritise 3–4 core customer types and write directly to their needs, fears and
desired outcomes.
06 — Plans & Pricing
Problem: A section is labelled “Plans & Pricing”, but visitors are shown plan names without actual prices.
This creates unnecessary friction and can make the section feel incomplete.
Recommended fix: Show clear pricing where appropriate, or rename the section and clearly explain how
pricing/trials work. Remove ambiguity.
07 — CTA Inconsistency
Problem: The website uses several variations: Book a Trial, Book Your Trial, Book Trial, Check
Availability, Chat Now, WhatsApp Now, etc. Multiple labels dilute the primary action.
Recommended fix: Use one primary CTA throughout: BOOK A TRIAL. Keep WhatsApp as the secondary
action.
08 — Testimonials
Problem: The testimonial section says “Real Results from Real Clients,” but the proof is weak without
stronger identity, photos, review context or links. Polished anonymous testimonials can feel
AI-generated even when genuine.
Recommended fix: Use real client names where permitted, photos, Google review excerpts/screenshots,
dates and specific experiences. Keep wording natural.
09 — Weak Social Proof
Problem: There is not enough visible evidence of demand or trust: ratings, review count, client stories,
credentials, results and real class moments should be more prominent.

Recommended fix: Build a trust stack: Google rating, review count, certifications, client stories, studio
photos and authentic class photography.
10 — Gallery
Problem: The gallery communicates that the studio has equipment and a physical space, but it does not
sell the experience strongly enough.
Recommended fix: Show people: Shilpa coaching, small groups, real movement, client interaction,
atmosphere, prenatal sessions and close-up details.
11 — Class Experience
Problem: A new visitor cannot quickly understand what their first session actually looks like. This is
especially important for beginners who may feel intimidated.
Recommended fix: Add a “Your First Session” 3–4 step section explaining what happens before, during
and after the class.
12 — Rehab & Prenatal
Problem: Rehab Pilates and Prenatal Pilates are high-trust services, but they are compressed into short
sections. These customers have more questions and need more reassurance.
Recommended fix: Create dedicated service pages with FAQs, credentials, suitability, process, safety
notes and strong booking CTAs.
13 — Missing FAQ
Problem: Common objections are left for WhatsApp: what to wear, beginner suitability, session duration,
class size, trial details, location, pricing, prenatal questions and more.
Recommended fix: Add a concise FAQ section and service-specific FAQs to reduce pre-booking friction.
14 — Location / Local SEO
Problem: Goregaon, Malad and Kandivali are mentioned, but the website could do much more to
convert local intent.
Recommended fix: Build dedicated local/service landing pages, improve location details, add
map/landmark information and strengthen local structured content.
15 — No Content Engine
Problem: There is little visible educational content to capture people searching for Pilates, posture, back
pain, prenatal movement, face yoga and related questions.
Recommended fix: Launch an SEO-focused blog/resource section and connect every article to a relevant
trial or consultation CTA.
16 — Visual Storytelling
Problem: The page reads as a sequence of information blocks rather than a brand story. The visitor is
told what the studio offers rather than shown what it feels like to be there.
Recommended fix: Rebuild the page narrative around: customer problem → Shilpa → method →
experience → proof → plans → booking.
17 — Premium Positioning
Problem: The combination of personal training, semi-private sessions, rehab, prenatal and face yoga
can support a premium position, but the current presentation feels closer to a generic local fitness
website.
